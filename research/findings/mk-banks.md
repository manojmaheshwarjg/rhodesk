# The Alternative: Traditional Bank Charters and Neo-ish Players a US Startup/SMB Would Otherwise Use

Research date: 2026-09-11. Independent research (WebSearch/WebFetch) plus cross-checks against the local Rho corpus at `scratchpad/rho/`.
Method note: wherever possible, fee numbers below come from the bank's own published fee schedule PDF (extracted with pdfminer and read directly), not from a review site. Where only secondary sources exist, that is said explicitly.
Convention: `[Rho claim]` marks a fact asserted only by Rho's marketing pages. Everything else is independently sourced with a URL and a date.

---

## 0. Executive summary of what the evidence actually shows

1. **The big four pay essentially nothing on operating cash.** Chase's own rate sheet in effect 2026-09-11 shows Chase Business Total Savings at **0.01% APY at every balance tier**, Chase Business Premier Savings at **0.02% relationship / 0.01% standard at every tier up to $10,000,000+**, and Chase Performance Business Checking with Interest at **0.01% APY on all balances**. Bank of America's Business Advantage Fundamentals and Relationship checking are both explicitly **"non-interest-bearing"** per its Feb 20, 2026 schedule. This is the single largest economic gap versus any yield-bearing fintech or digital bank, and it is verifiable from primary documents.
2. **Monthly fees are waivable but the waiver is a recurring balance tax.** Chase $15/$40/$95, BoA $16/$29.95, Wells Fargo $15/$25/$75 (the $75 Optimize tier has **no waiver available at all**). The waivers require $2,000 to $100,000 of dead balance depending on tier, re-qualified every statement period.
3. **Wire pricing at the big four is $25 to $50 outbound.** Chase $25 online / $35 branch domestic, $40 international USD online, $50 international at a branch. BoA $30 domestic out, $45 international USD out, $15 domestic in. Wells Fargo $30 digital / $40 branch outgoing.
4. **SVB is being retired as a brand in Q4 2026.** It is a division of First Citizens Bank; its technology/healthcare business becomes **First Citizens Innovation Banking** and Global Fund Banking becomes **First Citizens Fund Banking**. SVB's own published fee schedule is materially more expensive per unit than the marketing implies: **$125/month for SVB Online Banking**, plus **$25/month wire module** and **$40/month ACH module**, **$12 outgoing domestic wire**, **$25 outgoing USD international wire**, **$5 per same-day ACH**, **$10/month bill pay plus $0.40 per item over 15**.
5. **HSBC's US startup play is a 24-month fee holiday, then a very high balance bar.** The Spark package is $0 fees for 24 months including unlimited wires and ACH; the Series A+ Innovation Package waives the maintenance fee only at a **$1,800,000 combined average monthly balance**.
6. **Two of the "neo" names are actual chartered banks** (Grasshopper Bank, N.A. and Axos Bank) and four are not (Bluevine, Relay, Novo, Lili). That distinction is the whole counterparty-risk story.
7. **The 2023-2025 Synapse collapse is the case law.** ~$265M of end-user funds frozen across 100,000+ people; a shortfall the Chapter 11 trustee and the CFPB both put in the **$60M-$95M** range; the bankruptcy was **dismissed** in late 2025 without full reconciliation; the CFPB had to allocate **$46,248,291** from its Civil Penalty Fund on **2025-11-28** because there was no deposit-insurance mechanism that reached the victims. FDIC insurance never triggered, because **no bank failed**.
8. **The regulatory response is real but incomplete as of 2026-09-11.** The FDIC's custodial-account recordkeeping proposal (RIN 3064-AG07) is **still a proposal**, not a final rule, roughly two years after it was issued. The signage/misrepresentation rule (12 CFR Part 328) was finalized/amended on **2026-01-29** but its compliance date has been pushed to **2027-04-01**. So the disclosure regime that would have made the Synapse-style confusion illegal is not yet fully in force.

A startup parking $2M at Chase Business Total Savings earns roughly **$200/year**. The same $2M in a Treasury MMF sleeve at a fintech, or at Grasshopper's 3.00% savings tier, earns roughly **$60,000/year**. That delta, not the $15 monthly fee, is the real cost of a big-four operating account.

**What a startup actually gets at Chase:** branches (cash and coin handling, notarization, cashier's checks), free Positive Pay/ACH Debit Block, a national ACH/wire operation, the deepest credit card lineup, and treasury services that scale into Analysis Business Checking with an earnings credit offset. Chase's AP/expense answer for SMBs is **Cashflow360**, a white-labeled BILL product with unpublished bundle pricing [Rho claim].

**Conspicuously not stated by Chase:** an SMB-tier banking API for Business Complete accounts; any published bill-pay/AP bundle price; ACH origination pricing on the consumer-facing product pages (it is buried on p.18 of a 25-page PDF).

## PART E. WHAT A REAL BANK CHARTER PROVIDES THAT A FINTECH DOES NOT

1. **A direct depositor relationship and a direct claim.** At a chartered bank, the customer is the depositor of record. FDIC insurance attaches to a deposit account in the customer's name, at a named bank, with the bank's own records establishing ownership. At a fintech, the customer is typically a beneficial owner of a pooled custodial (FBO) account, and insurance is **pass-through**, which per FDIC guidance (https://www.fdic.gov/consumer-resource-center/2024-06/banking-third-party-apps, published **2024-05-31**) requires that **"Records must be kept to identify who owns the money and the specific amount that each person owns,"** plus "other requirements as well." If the records are wrong, the coverage is contested.
2. **Resolution mechanics.** A failed insured bank goes into **FDIC receivership**, and insured depositors are typically made whole within one business day. A failed fintech goes into **bankruptcy**, where the customer is a creditor in a queue, subject to a trustee, automatic stay, professional fees, and years of delay. Synapse proved this empirically: the Chapter 11 case ran **19 months** and was **dismissed** without full reconciliation.
3. **Balance-sheet lending.** Venture debt, capital-call lines, equipment finance, CRE, and traditional working-capital lending require regulatory capital and a charter. SVB/First Citizens writes venture debt at **20-40% of the last equity round with a $4M minimum round** [Rho claim, svb.com]; First Citizens' Global Fund Banking pipeline was **$11.5 billion at year-end 2025**. No fintech can replicate this; fintech "capital" products are either bank-funded, warehouse-funded, or receivables-based.
4. **Payment-system access in its own name.** A chartered bank holds a Federal Reserve master account, its own ABA routing number, direct Fedwire/FedACH/FedNow access, and can be a direct Nacha ODFI. A fintech rides someone else's rails and inherits that bank's risk appetite, cutoffs, and limits. When a partner bank gets a consent order (see Thread), the fintech's payment behavior changes.
5. **Discount window and liquidity backstop.** Only insured depository institutions can borrow from the Fed's discount window or (for members) use standing facilities. This is what a "fortress balance sheet" actually means operationally.
6. **Prudential supervision of the entity holding your money.** Chase, BoA, Wells Fargo, First Citizens, HSBC USA, Grasshopper and Axos are examined by the OCC/Fed/FDIC for capital, liquidity, asset quality, and BSA/AML. Bluevine, Relay, Novo, Lili and Rho are **not** prudentially supervised as depositories. Their partner banks are, and the examination pressure flows to them, not to the fintech.
7. **Branches, cash, and physical instruments.** Cash and coin deposits, cashier's checks, medallion signature guarantees, notarization, safe deposit. Rho concedes this directly: BoA's free cash allowance is "real, tangible value that a fully digital platform simply can't offer," and "Rho doesn't support cash deposits at all."
8. **Analysis/earnings-credit accounting.** Chase, Wells Fargo, SVB and HSBC all offer analyzed checking where balances generate an earnings credit that offsets service fees (Chase ECR **0.20%** to **0.30%** as of 2026-09-11; Wells Fargo Optimize and Analyzed accounts carry an earnings allowance). This is how large cash balances are actually monetized at a bank, and it has no fintech equivalent.
9. **Longevity and brand durability for governance.** A board, an auditor, an acquirer's diligence team, and a large enterprise customer's vendor-risk questionnaire all react differently to "our operating account is at JPMorgan Chase" than to "our operating account is at a fintech whose partner bank was acquired in August."

**The honest counter-list, which the fee tables above support:** a charter buys none of the software. Chase has no SMB banking API and sells AP through a white-labeled BILL product. SVB charges **$125/month just to log in**, plus **$25/month for wires** and **$40/month for ACH**, plus **$50-$250/month for bill pay**. BoA and Wells Fargo publish no ACH origination pricing at the SMB tier. And all of them pay **0.01%-0.02%** on business savings while T-bills yield multiples of that. **The startup trade is: charter-grade safety and credit versus software, yield, and per-unit payment cost.**

**Headline numbers to carry:**
- **100,000+ people** lost access to **over $265 million** across several fintech platforms.
- **Shortfall between the ledger and the cash: $65M-$96M** per the trustee; **$60M-$90M** per the CFPB. **Nobody knows the exact number**; McWilliams stated that "a full reconciliation to the last dollar with the Synapse ledger" may not be possible.
- **~$219 million** was held by partner banks as of May 2024, most of which has been distributed.
- **CFPB allocation: $46,248,291**, against a shortfall of $60M-$90M. **The federal backstop covered roughly half to three-quarters of the gap, and it came from a civil penalty fund, not deposit insurance.**
- **Zero dollars of FDIC deposit insurance were paid out**, because **no insured bank failed**. Evolve, Lineage, American Bank and AMG National Trust all survived.

### F2. What Synapse actually taught

1. **FDIC insurance is not a fintech guarantee.** The FDIC's own consumer guidance says it plainly: **"FDIC deposit insurance does not protect against the insolvency or bankruptcy of a nonbank company."** Pass-through coverage protects against the failure of the **bank**, not against the failure, fraud, or ledger mismanagement of the **intermediary**.
2. **The failure mode is reconciliation, not insolvency.** The money was not "stolen" in a way anyone has proven. It was **unreconcilable**: the program's ledger and the banks' records disagreed, and nobody had an authoritative source of truth. This is exactly the risk that a chartered bank's own books eliminate by construction.
3. **A middleware layer multiplies the problem.** Synapse was not the consumer brand and not the bank. It was the ledger in between. Relay's structure today (**Relay -> Unit -> Thread Bank -> program banks**) has the same topology.
4. **The partner bank's regulatory state is your regulatory state.** Evolve took a Fed C&D in June 2024. Thread took an FDIC consent order in May 2024. Blue Ridge, Choice Financial, Piermont, Lineage and others were hit across 2023-2024 (see the Banking Dive running list). **A fintech's deposit product can be constrained or unwound by an enforcement action against a bank the customer never chose.**
5. **Recovery is slow, partial, and political.** Nineteen months to a dismissal. A CFPB civil-penalty-fund allocation as the actual remedy. State regulators (California DFPI) doing customer notice in 2026. **A startup with payroll due on Friday cannot survive this timeline.**
6. **Sweep networks are a real benefit and a separate risk.** Spreading $5M across 20+ institutions genuinely extends coverage. It also means twenty-plus reconciliation relationships, and coverage that only works if the placement records are correct at every institution. Bluevine's own disclosure concedes that in a failure you would need to **"go directly to our partner bank"** to access balances.

### F3. Practical counterparty questions a startup should ask any fintech

Grounded in what FDIC guidance actually tells consumers to verify (https://www.fdic.gov/consumer-resource-center/2024-06/banking-third-party-apps, 2024-05-31: **"You should identify the specific FDIC-insured bank or banks where they say they will deposit your funds"** and confirm via **BankFind**):

1. Name every insured institution that can hold my money, today, in writing. (Rho publishes Webster/Santander for checking but says the savings network list is **"available from Rho support on request."** Bluevine publishes Coastal Community Bank and puts the program-bank list in a support article. Relay publishes Thread Bank. Novo publishes Middlesex Federal Savings, F.A. Lili publishes Sunrise Banks, N.A.)
2. Is the account titled as a custodial/FBO account, and are ownership records maintained by the bank, by the fintech, or by a middleware provider?
3. Is there a middleware layer (Unit, Synctera, Treasury Prime, a successor to Synapse)? If yes, who holds the authoritative ledger?
4. Who reconciles daily, and against what?
5. Is any partner bank under a public enforcement action? (FDIC, Fed and OCC enforcement actions are public and searchable.)
6. If the fintech shuts down tomorrow, what is the documented process by which I reach the bank directly?
7. Is my "yield" a deposit rate at an insured bank, or a securities product? (Rho Treasury, Mercury Treasury and Brex Treasury are **securities, SIPC not FDIC**. Every provider in this document that offers >3% on large balances is either a chartered bank paying a deposit rate, a subscription tier, or a securities sleeve.)
8. What happens to my money in the 24 hours between a deposit hitting the fintech and the sweep executing? (FDIC: funds sent to a non-bank are not eligible for insurance **"until the company deposits them in an FDIC-insured bank and after other conditions are met."**)

## PART G. 2024-2026 REGULATORY RESPONSE, AND WHAT IS STILL MISSING

### G1. FDIC Recordkeeping for Custodial Accounts (RIN 3064-AG07): STILL A PROPOSAL

- Issued **2024-09-17**, published **2024-10-02**, comment period extended to **2025-01-16**.
- Would require FDIC-insured banks holding **"custodial deposit accounts with transactional features"** to maintain records sufficient to determine the individual owner of the funds, **including a requirement to reconcile the account for each individual owner on a daily basis**; and to **either maintain the beneficial-owner ledger themselves or have unrestricted access to the third party's ledger**.
- FDIC Chairman Gruenberg framed it around Synapse directly: the point is that **"banks know the actual owner of deposits placed in a bank by a third party such as Synapse, whether the deposit has actually been placed in the banks."**
- **It was NOT among the four proposals the FDIC Board rescinded on 2025-03-03.** As of this research (2026-09-11) I found **no final rule and no withdrawal**. Analysis from Sullivan & Cromwell, Mayer Brown, Alston & Bird, KPMG and CSBS all describes it in the proposed posture, and BPI, the American Fintech Council and the Financial Technology Association all filed comments.
- **This is the most important gap.** Roughly two years after the Synapse collapse, the single rule that would have directly prevented it is still unfinalized. A startup evaluating a fintech in September 2026 **cannot rely on a federal daily-reconciliation mandate**, because there isn't one.

### G2. FDIC Part 328: signage and misrepresentation of insured status

- **Final rule adopted 2023-12-20**, amending 12 CFR Part 328 (FDIC Official Signs and Advertising Requirements, False Advertising, Misrepresentation of Insured Status, and Misuse of the FDIC's Name or Logo).
- **Subpart B is the part that binds fintechs.** Per **12 CFR 328.102**, it is prohibited for a non-bank to make a statement about deposit insurance that **fails to clearly and conspicuously disclose that the person is not an FDIC-insured depository institution and that FDIC insurance only covers the failure of the FDIC-insured depository institution**; and it is prohibited for a non-bank to represent or imply that an advertised product is FDIC-insured while failing to **"clearly and conspicuously identify the insured depository institution(s) with which the representing party has a direct or indirect business relationship for the placement of deposits."** Where deposits and non-deposit products appear together, the presentation must **"clearly and conspicuously differentiate between insured deposits and Non-Deposit Products by disclosing that Non-Deposit Products: are not insured by the FDIC; are not deposits; and may lose value."** Statements about pass-through coverage must **"clearly and conspicuously disclose that certain conditions must be satisfied for pass-through deposit insurance coverage to apply."** (Sources: https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/12/328.102 and https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-12/chapter-III/subchapter-B/part-328/subpart-B)
- **Compliance timeline has slipped twice.** The initial compliance date for the digital sign requirements was **2025-05-01**; the FDIC Board delayed it on **2025-03-03**; the new date became **2026-03-01**; the FDIC then proposed further amendments (Federal Register **2025-11-28**, document 2025-21461) and issued a **final rule published 2026-01-29** (document 2026-01806), **effective 2026-03-02**, with **compliance required by 2027-04-01**.
- What the January 2026 final rule does: addresses implementation issues and confusion from the 2023 signage requirements, provides additional flexibility to insured depository institutions, requires the official digital sign on the homepage or initial page of a website or application, specifies the sign's text be navy blue or black in Source Sans Pro Web or similar, and clarifies that the rule **does not prohibit including uninsured products on digital pages bearing the FDIC official digital sign** so long as the required differentiation is made.
- **Net effect for 2026:** the substantive prohibitions in Subpart B (misrepresentation, failure to name the bank, failure to disclose pass-through conditions) are **already law** and enforceable, but the granular digital-signage mechanics do not bite until **2027-04-01**.

### G3. Interagency action on bank-fintech arrangements

- **2024-07-25:** Federal Reserve, OCC and FDIC issue a **joint statement** on banks' arrangements with third parties to deliver deposit products and services, flagging **operational, compliance, strategic, liquidity and concentration risks**, plus **consumer protection, end-user confusion, and misrepresentation of deposit insurance coverage**. OCC Bulletin 2024-20; Fed SR 24-5 / CA 24-5.
- Same day: a **Request for Information** on bank-fintech arrangements (OCC Bulletin 2024-21), comments due **2024-09-30**. Questions included whether the agencies' categorization of arrangements was adequate and whether these partnerships **reduce or increase instability** in the financial system.
- **No rulemaking has issued from the RFI as of 2026-09-11** in what I could find. The joint statement is guidance, not binding.

## PART I. CONTRADICTIONS, AMBIGUITIES, AND CONSPICUOUS SILENCES

**Contradictions found:**
1. **Chase's own ACH Payments line is internally inconsistent.** "$2.50 per item for the first 10 items per month" (ACH Payments) vs. "$25 for the first 25 items per month" (ACH Collections). The parallel structure implies both are monthly bundles; the literal text of the first is per-item. Chase's Effective 11/16/2025 schedule, p.18.
2. **Rho's own SVB page states two different Treasury yields.** The comparison table says "Up to 4.66% net (as of 09/11/2026)"; the FAQ on the same page says "up to 4.55% as of 08/02/2026"; the footnote qualifies 4.66% as assuming **$20M+ of total Rho deposits, 100% allocation to VFSTX, and a 0.15% fee tier**. The headline number is achievable only by the largest customers.
3. **Rho's HSBC page compares against HSBC's personal Premier Checking terms** ($50/month, $75K balance or $5K direct deposit) while titling the page "Rho vs HSBC Innovation Banking." Those are different products. The page also carries **no as-of date and no source footnote**, unlike Rho's Chase and SVB pages.
4. **"FDIC- and NCUA-insured institutions."** Rho headlines "$75M in FDIC insurance" while its own help text says the network is "400+ FDIC- **and NCUA**-insured institutions." NCUA is a separate insurance fund for credit unions.
5. **Novo's outgoing wire fee** is reported as $25, $30, and "up to $45" by different 2026 secondary sources. Novo's own fee-schedule page serves a PDF dated 2026-08-26 and no HTML fee table.
6. **Axos Basic Business Checking minimum opening deposit**: Axos's own product page says "No initial deposit or minimum balance requirements"; a secondary review says "$1,000 minimum initial deposit."
7. **Relay's savings APY** (1.11% / 1.75% / 3.00% by plan) does not match **Thread Bank's own published tiered APY** effective 1/5/2026 (e.g. 0.67% on $100K-$200K). The plan rate is a Relay program construct on top of the bank rate.

**Conspicuously NOT stated:**
- **SVB/First Citizens publishes no account prices at all** on its business checking page and no current startup money-market rate sheet. The last public one Rho could find was dated **2025-12-10**.
- **HSBC Innovation Banking publishes no fee schedule, no interest rate, and no FDIC coverage limit** for either Spark or the Innovation Package.
- **Bank of America's Business Schedule of Fees contains no ACH origination, same-day ACH, or RTP pricing whatsoever.**
- **Lili publishes no wire, ACH, or cash-deposit fees on its plans page.**
- **Bluevine does not publish the number or identity of its program banks on the public FDIC page.**
- **Rho does not publish its savings network bank list** ("available from Rho support on request"), notwithstanding 12 CFR 328.102's requirement that a non-bank representing FDIC insurance "clearly and conspicuously identify the insured depository institution(s)."
- **None of the fintechs publish what happens to funds in transit** between receipt and sweep placement, which is precisely the window FDIC guidance flags as uninsured.
- **No provider in this document publishes a daily reconciliation attestation**, because no rule requires one.
