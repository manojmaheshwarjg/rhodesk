## 0. Headline findings (read this first)

1. **Rho has filed no SEC Form D since January 2022.** Under Technologies, Inc. (CIK 0001756460) has exactly five Form D filings, 2018 through 2022, totalling **$104,732,452** of equity sold. There is no Form D for the widely repeated "$150M Series C in 2024 led by Balderton Capital." That claim appears only on AI-generated lead-gen sites and should be treated as **unverified and probably false**. [VERIFIED-PRIMARY]
2. **The "Webster Bank, a division of Santander Bank, N.A." construction is real and recent.** Webster Bank, National Association (FDIC cert 18221) **ceased to exist as a separately insured institution on 08/20/2026** (FDIC `ENDEFYMD` = 08/20/2026, `ACTIVE` = 0). Its business was merged into Santander Bank, N.A. (FDIC cert 29950), which retains the "Webster Bank" name as a division/brand. Rho's disclosure is technically accurate. [VERIFIED-PRIMARY, FDIC BankFind, index built 2026-09-11]
3. **Rho retroactively rewrote history.** Rho's 2022-era blog post now reads "Sterling merged with Webster Bank, a division of Santander Bank, N.A." for a January 2022 event, four and a half years before Santander existed in that role. A site-wide find-and-replace created anachronisms across historical content.
4. **Hard, independently verifiable scale number:** Rho Treasury (RBB Treasury LLC) reported **$1,892,904,589 in regulatory AUM across 1,078 accounts** in its Form ADV annual amendment filed 03/25/2026 (fiscal year end December, so as of 12/31/2025). 1,071 of those accounts are "corporations or other businesses" ($1,886,487,962); 7 are charitable organizations ($6,416,627). [VERIFIED-PRIMARY, SEC IAPD]
5. **Rho's own corpus contradicts itself on its headline scale metric.** One page says "8,000 businesses with **$4B+ in deposits**"; a dozen other pages say "8,000+ customers moving more than **$4 billion monthly**." Those are different claims off the same number (a stock vs. a flow, differing by roughly 12x annualized). Neither is independently verifiable.
6. **Rho used Evolve Bank & Trust as a banking partner and never says so.** Banking Dive, 08/23/2022: "It partners with Evolve Bank & Trust and Webster Bank to deliver its products." Rho's current marketing attacks Mercury specifically for its Evolve relationship and the June 2024 Evolve breach. Rho's own Evolve history appears nowhere in the 128-page core corpus, the 269-page help corpus, or the 125-post comparison-blog corpus.
7. **Zero CFPB complaints, by design.** A search of the CFPB Consumer Complaint Database for "Rho," "Rho Technologies," "Rho Business Banking," and "Under Technologies" returns **0 hits** in every field. This is not evidence of customer satisfaction: Rho's Terms of Service explicitly bar "individual consumers," so its customers fall outside CFPB jurisdiction. By contrast, Webster Bank, N.A. has **1,628** all-time complaints, 367 of them filed since 01/01/2025. [VERIFIED-PRIMARY]
8. **Rho's public status page has been effectively dead since September 2024.** Nine total entries ever; the last incident was 09/05/2023; the last entry of any kind was a planned database maintenance on 09/28/2024. Either two years of flawless uptime or an abandoned status page. The page currently reports "All Systems Operational" (snapshot 2026-09-11T19:13:30-04:00).
9. **Trustpilot is 3.4/5 from 53 reviews, with 26% one-star**, and Trustpilot flags that Rho "hasn't replied to negative reviews." Rho's own About page advertises "Google 4.8 ★★★★★" and never mentions Trustpilot.
10. **Co-founder Alex Wheldon appears to have left.** His LinkedIn headline is "Charles Square Ventures." TheOrg's current Rho leadership card lists Cook, a CFO, COO, CCO, CBDO and Chief Credit Officer, with no Wheldon. Not confirmed by any Rho or press statement. Third co-founder Peter Damian Kimmelman was an executive officer and director on the 2018 Form D and gone by the August 2019 filing.

### 6.5 Conspicuous omissions in Rho's own material

1. **Evolve Bank & Trust is never mentioned as a Rho partner anywhere in the 522-page local corpus.** It is mentioned five times in the corpus, all of them as a *criticism of Mercury* ("experiencing a data breach via partner Evolve Bank & Trust in 2024"; "Mercury completed its migration away from Evolve Bank & Trust to Choice Financial Group and Column N.A." in March 2025; "has faced challenges with banking partners that are under regulatory scrutiny"). 2. **The FDIC six-month separate-insurance window after a bank merger is never mentioned.** Webster's own FAQ states that deposits held at Webster and at Santander as of the merger date "receive separate insurance coverage for at least six months, potentially longer for CDs." 3. **Rho markets partner-bank size as a safety differentiator while running its newest credit product through Lead Bank ($2.76B)**, smaller than three of the four competitor banks it disparages by size.
4. **No disclosure of when or why the Evolve relationship ended, or whether any customer deposits moved.**

### 6.6 The retroactive rewrite

Rho's blog post `rho-and-webster-bank`, describing events of 2020 to 2022, currently reads:

Both statements are anachronistic: in 2021 and January 2022 the entity was Webster Bank, N.A., a standalone national bank; the Santander division only came into existence on 08/20/2026. The rewrite is cosmetic rather than deceptive in substance, but it does mean **no page on rho.co can be trusted as a record of what the bank relationship looked like at any past date.**

## 13. The critical view, assembled

1. **No fresh equity since October 2021.** Five years is a long time for a fintech with 337 employees, nine product lines, and a stated ambition to serve companies "from day zero to IPO." Either Rho is capital-efficient and near breakeven (plausible: interchange-led, no subscription costs, offshore engineering in Belgrade and Poland, no marquee marketing spend), or it has been unable to price a round it likes. The Forge mark of ~$440M against a 2021 Series B suggests the latter is at least partly true.
2. **Interchange concentration.** Cook to Banking Dive (2022): Rho "generates a significant proportion of its revenue from interchange fees ... The company doesn't charge subscription fees." Rho's entire marketing position is $0 fees at every tier. That makes revenue a function of card spend plus deposit net interest margin plus the 15-60 bps treasury fee. A rate-cut cycle compresses two of three legs simultaneously.
3. **Bank-partner concentration risk that Rho itself argues is a virtue.** All checking deposits and all card issuance sit with one sponsor bank, which has just been absorbed into a foreign-owned banking group in a $12.2B transaction. Post-integration, Santander may reassess a BaaS/sponsor program it did not originate. Rho's contingency is undisclosed. Rho's disclosure that "If Rho ceased operating, deposits remain at the banks holding them" answers the wrong question: the live risk is the sponsor bank exiting, not Rho failing.
4. **The FDIC aggregation cliff.** After roughly 02/20/2027, Rho checking deposits are insured at Santander Bank, N.A., not at a separate Webster. Any customer with Santander or Openbank exposure aggregates into one $250,000 limit. Rho does not mention this anywhere.
5. **Onboarding and offboarding are the complaint cluster.** Four of the four negative Trustpilot reviews captured are compliance-driven: two account closures/denials with no stated reason, one $300,000 hold "for weeks without any explanation," one support-quality complaint. The two vacant senior compliance roles and two open Financial Crime Analyst reqs suggest Rho knows this.
6. **No escalation path.** No CFPB jurisdiction, $500 liability cap, confidential individual arbitration in New York, one-year limitations period, termination "for no reason." A customer with $300,000 frozen has a Trustpilot review and a small-claims filing as their practical options.
7. **The status page is dead.** No verifiable uptime record since September 2024.
8. **Press silence since 2022.** No independent journalism has examined Rho's numbers in four years. Every 2026 scale figure in circulation traces back to rho.co.

## 14. The positive view, assembled

1. **The regulatory record is genuinely clean.** No enforcement actions found at any regulator. No federal litigation. No disciplinary disclosures on the SEC-registered subsidiary. No data breach found. For a seven-year-old fintech holding other companies' money, that is a real asset.
2. **The bank partner is, in fact, dramatically larger than every peer's.** Even corrected to bank level, Santander Bank, N.A. at ~$104B (pre-merger) or ~$190B (combined) dwarfs Column ($1.77B), Coastal ($5.45B), First Internet ($5.53B), Choice ($6.38B) and Cross River ($8.53B). The strategic claim survives the correction to the arithmetic.
3. **Rho Treasury's $1.89B AUM across 1,078 business accounts is a real, SEC-attested scale number**, and the 0.15%-0.60% fee schedule and $50K minimum published in marketing match the Form ADV exactly. Rho's most checkable public claim checks out.

8. **Pricing is genuinely zero-fee** on the core: $0 same-day ACH, $0 domestic wires, $0 subscription, $0 per-user, $0 minimum, $0 wire recall. The only standard fees are 1% FX conversion and an optional $15 SWIFT fee.
